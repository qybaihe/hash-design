Skerrow IslandCauseway, 2027

A one-day music festival on Skerrow Island, for nine hundred people. The island is reached on foot across 1.2 km of tidal causeway, which opens twice that day: once to let everyone over, once to let everyone back. The tide sets the hours.

By the Skerrow Island Trust · Photographs: none on file

Skerrow Island is reached on foot. The way over is a causeway 1.2 km long, which the tide covers and uncovers; parts of it are wet sand, parts of it seaweed. On Saturday 19 June 2027 it is the way in to Causeway, a one-day music festival for nine hundred people, first held in 2024 and now in its fourth year.

The causeway is open for arrivals from 09:40 to 13:10, and for departures from 22:15 to 01:30. These are that day’s tide times. Nobody crosses outside those windows; the harbour boat is for emergencies only.

Everything else on the island has been fitted around them. The music is on two stages, the Boathouse and the Field. The last set ends at 21:45, so that everyone reaches the causeway in time.

Visitors wear boots.

Two stages

Boathouse & Field

There are two stages. The Boathouse is indoors and holds 220 people. The Field is outdoors. Eight acts are listed, four to each.

On the Field: The Lantern Choir, Mara Quist, Seven Herons and Low Orbit Brass. In the Boathouse: Ada Fenwick, solo cello; Tomasz & the Weir; Kelp; and the Ruth Ono Trio. Set times are not on file.

Arrivals close at 13:10 and departures open at 22:15. In between, the causeway is shut, and the island keeps whoever is on it.

“Nobody crosses outside those windows; the harbour boat is for emergencies only.”

The last set ends at 21:45, so that everyone reaches the causeway in time. Thirty minutes separate the final note from the first departure. The window then stays open for three and a quarter hours, until 01:30.

Everyone leaves the way they came: on foot, over the sand.

No single-use plastic is allowed on the island. Bring a cup, or buy an enamel one there for £6. It is yours to keep.

Wear boots. Parts of the causeway are wet sand and seaweed, and the crossing is made twice: once in daylight, once at night.

Food comes from two island kitchens. Both take cash and card.

Tickets go on sale on 1 March 2027: £54 for adults, £27 for ages 12 to 17, free for children under 12. Capacity is nine hundred.

Causeway is organised by the Skerrow Island Trust, a volunteer charity. The festival was first held in 2024; this is its fourth year.

Profits go to repairing the sea wall.■
