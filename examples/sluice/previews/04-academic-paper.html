Sluice: a self-hosted inbox for webhooks

One delivery, logged from T − 5 min to T + 30 d

Ines Okafor

maintainer, with 41 contributors

Version 0.9.2  ·  released 14 October 2026  ·  Go  ·  MIT licence

Abstract. Sluice gives you a public URL for webhooks, stores every delivery, and forwards it to your local or internal service. It is one static binary of about 11 MB (§2.1) and keeps its state in a single SQLite file (§2.2). We follow one delivery along one time axis, T being the moment it arrives. At the door its HMAC-SHA256 signature is verified, and anything older than 5 minutes is turned away (§3). At T it is stored and forwarded (§4). A failed forward is retried with exponential backoff for up to 72 hours (§5). For 30 days by default it can be searched and replayed (§6). The project’s own benchmark records 1,800 deliveries per second on a 2-vCPU VM [2]. No hosted service, no account, no telemetry.

Keywords: webhooks · SQLite · HMAC-SHA256 · exponential backoff · Go

The job description is three clauses long. Sluice gives you a public URL for webhooks; it stores every delivery; it forwards each one to your local or internal service. Nothing is hosted for you, there is no account to open and there is no telemetry to switch off (§8).

1.1Notation and plan

We write T for the moment a delivery reaches its public URL and put every event on one axis relative to it: minutes before, hours after, days after. Figure 3 draws that axis end to end; Table 2 keeps it as a log, one event code per line. 3T − 5 min to T: the door

A delivery gets in only if both halves of Eq. (1) hold: its HMAC-SHA256 signature verifies, and it is no more than 5 minutes old when it reaches the URL.

accept(d)⟺hmac-sha256(d) verifies and T − tsent(d) ≤ 5 min (1)

Anything older is rejected, whatever its signature says (Fig. 1). Of the whole axis in Fig. 3, this is the only interval that lies before T; everything after it happens to a delivery that has already been let in.

4At T: stored, then forwarded

At T the delivery is stored — every delivery is, whether or not the forward succeeds — and it is forwarded to the address given with --forward. In Listing 1 that is http://localhost:3000; it may be any local or internal service.

5T to T + 72 h: retries

If the forward fails, Sluice tries again, with exponential backoff, for up to 72 hours. Each wait is longer than the one before it, and the last retry falls no later than the bound in Eq. (2).

tk ≤ T + 72 h for every retry k (2)

The ceiling is the fact on record; the individual intervals are not stated, so Fig. 2 draws them schematically rather than to scale. After the last retry the delivery is still stored, and §6.2 applies.

6T to T + 30 d: search, replay, retention

6.1Search

The web UI runs on port 8081. Deliveries can be searched by path, by header or by body text, for as long as they are kept (§6.3).

6.2Replay

Any stored delivery can be sent again with the command in Listing 2.

6Replay is for stored deliveries, so its window closes when the delivery is removed: Eq. (3).

Because replay works on whatever is stored, its window is the retention period6 of §6.3.

6.3Retention

Deliveries are kept for 30 days by default, Eq. (3). The --retain flag changes that number, and the right-hand end of the axis in Fig. 3 moves with it.

tdel = T + r, r = 30 d unless set with --retain (3)

8Availability
