01 NAME

sluice — a self-hosted inbox for webhooks

02 SYNOPSIS

sluice serve --forward url [--retain period]
sluice replay delivery-id

03 DESCRIPTION

Hello —

I'm Ines Okafor, and I maintain Sluice. Let me tell you why it exists. In 2023 I lost a weekend of payment webhooks, and afterwards I started writing the tool I wish I'd had.

So here is what it does for you. Sluice gives you a public URL for your webhooks. It stores every delivery that arrives, and it forwards each one to your local or internal service. When a forward fails, it tries again with exponential backoff, for up to 72 hours. And any delivery it has stored, you can replay.

It is small: one static binary, about 11 MB, for Linux, macOS and Windows on amd64 and arm64. Everything is kept in a single SQLite file, so there is no Redis, no Postgres and no queue for you to run.

When you want to look back, the web UI on port 8081 lets you search deliveries by path, header or body text. They stay for 30 days unless you change that with --retain.

Installing takes one line and starting takes another; both are under EXAMPLES. I hope it saves you a weekend.

04 COMMANDS

serve
Take webhooks at your public URL, store every delivery, and forward it to the service you name with --forward.
replay delivery-id
Forward one stored delivery again. Any delivery Sluice has kept can be replayed.

05 OPTIONS

--forward url
Your local or internal service, for example http://localhost:3000. If a forward fails, Sluice retries with exponential backoff for up to 72 hours.
--retain period
How long deliveries are kept. Leave it out and Sluice keeps them for 30 days.

06 SECURITY

Sluice verifies HMAC-SHA256 signatures, and it rejects deliveries older than 5 minutes.

There is no hosted service, no account to create and no telemetry. You run it, and it reports to no one.

08 EXAMPLES

Install:

$ curl -fsSL https://sluice.example/install.sh | sh

Start, forwarding to a service on port 3000:

$ sluice serve --forward http://localhost:3000

Send a stored delivery again:

$ sluice replay <delivery-id>

Then open the web UI on port 8081 and search by path, header or body text.

09 NOTES

In the project's own benchmark, Sluice takes 1,800 deliveries per second on a 2-vCPU VM. It is our own benchmark, so please measure on your machines before you lean on it.
